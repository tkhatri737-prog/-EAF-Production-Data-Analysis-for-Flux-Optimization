# Reduction in Flux Consumption at EAF

Internship project on lowering lime and dolomite use in the electric arc furnace (EAF) at Jindal Steel Limited, Raigarh.

The full study is in [Reduction in flux consumption at EAF.pptx](Reduction%20in%20flux%20consumption%20at%20EAF.pptx).

## Objective

Reduce flux consumption in the EAF while keeping slag chemistry and steel quality on target.

The furnace produces about 100 tonnes of liquid steel per heat, with about 33 heats per day and a tap-to-tap time of about 40 minutes. Charge is scrap, hot metal, and continuous DRI, with lime and dolomite as fluxes.

## What the study covers

- Plant visits (EAF, DRI plant, and lime and dolomite plant) and process mapping
- Heat-level data analysis (about 3,000 heats, 12 May to 12 June, plus monthly trends)
- Flux requirement calculation from charge chemistry and target steel grade
- An Excel EAF flux calculator and a proposed change to the flux-addition SOP
- Slag, lime, and DRI sampling, including ways to improve lime reactivity
- Literature review of WDXRF, LIBS slag basicity, and pneumatic lime injection

## Main findings

- Average overall flux consumption was about **133 kg/t**, with monthly averages of about 128–136 kg/t and a maximum of about **217 kg/t**.
- Higher DRI weight is associated with higher flux use, but heat-to-heat scatter is large because input chemistry varies.
- Hot metal silicon, not hot metal weight alone, drives flux demand. Higher Si means less hot metal is charged and more flux is needed.
- Scrap shows only a weak link with flux, and it is also adjusted for low-phosphorus grades.
- DRI metallization, Fe(M), is not a stable predictor by itself. July data showed lower flux at higher Fe(M), while May and June did not.
- Lime use averaged about **131 kg/t** of steel, ranging from about 100 to 178 kg/t. Operators do not see real-time DRI gangue (SiO2, Al2O3), so flux additions and slag basicity vary from heat to heat.
- Lime reactivity can fall after handling, storage, and exposure, which raises the amount needed to hit the same slag chemistry.

## Recommendations

1. **WDXRF for DRI impurities.** Wet chemical analysis takes about 6–7 hours. WDXRF can report oxide impurities in about 20–25 minutes so flux can be set from the actual gangue. It is not proposed for accurate metallic iron.
2. **Flux calculator and a fixed SOP.** Estimate lime and dolomite from hot metal, DRI, and scrap chemistry and the target grade, then trim the rate from metal samples and slag condition.
3. **Pneumatic fine-lime injection.** Literature on multipoint lime injection showed lower lime use with refining performance held. A plant-scale estimate in the deck, using a partial realization of that saving, is about **Rs. 4.60 crore per year**.
4. **In-furnace basicity check (LIBS)** and a preference for fresh, soft-burnt in-house lime over aged purchased lime.
